Membership Form Playbook

Every field, option, and setting for building the membership signup in the ChabadOne form builder — in the order you'll click them.

Prices live in the text

The builder can't calculate, so the pricing table sits in the form's intro and the member types their own first payment from it. Simple, and it works.

The form bills the schedule

The payment section lets the member choose monthly or annual billing at checkout, and the gateway renews it automatically. The office's job is verifying the typed amount matches the table (step 9).

Blank fields disappear

Empty fields are dropped from the notification email and the receipt, so one form serves every household. Spouse and children details ARE required when they exist — the labels say so, and the office verifies (step 9), since the builder can't enforce it per household.

1

Create the form page

Make a new page of the same type as the High Holiday reservations page (/546190) — that's the page type that has the form builder plus a Form Settings area for payment, notifications, and the receipt.

  • Page title: Membership — set it in the page settings right away (the HH page sat as "Untitled" for a while).
  • If there's no obvious "add form page" option, duplicating the HH form page and removing its fields gets you the same thing.

Add the membership fields

Field labels become the row names in the notification email and the receipt — so type them exactly as written here. Three kinds of rows: Required for everyone, Optional for everyone, and Required if it applies — mandatory for the households they apply to, but left un-ticked in the builder so other households can still submit (how that's enforced is in step 5).

Add the family fields

Spouse and children details are mandatory for the households that have them — a couple must give their spouse's details, a family must list every child. Add a text block above this group that says exactly that:

Spouse EmailOptionalText box
Spouse BirthdayCouples & familiesText box — help text: MM/DD/YYYY — required for couples and two-parent families.
AnniversaryCouples & familiesText box — help text: MM/DD/YYYY — required for married couples.
Child 1FamiliesText box — help text: Name & birthday — every child at home must be listed. One box per child keeps the form short; repeat for Child 2 through Child 6.
Anything ElseOptionalParagraph text — help text: Hebrew names, yahrzeits, how you'd like to be listed on the donor wall, questions for the Rabbi — anything we should know. This one field replaces a page of questionnaire; the Rabbi follows up personally.
Why these stay un-ticked in the builder: ticking Required would demand a spouse from singles too — the builder can't make a field required for one household type only. So the requirement is enforced three ways: the labels and the text block above ask for it plainly, the office checks every couple/family signup and collects anything missing before setting up billing (step 9), and once the form is live the site's script layer can enforce it automatically in the browser — blocking submission when a couple or family leaves spouse or child boxes empty. Send the page URL when it's published and that enforcement gets wired in.
6

Billing & payment

Membership AmountRequired The payment Amount field (use the builder's own amount field if payment settings provide one). Help text: From the table above — your monthly amount if you choose monthly billing, or 12x if you choose annual.

Payment settings

  • Enable credit card payment with the same gateway the High Holiday form uses — the card number, expiration, and CVV fields come with it.
  • Turn on recurrence and offer Monthly and Annually. If the settings allow it, turn one-time payment off — a membership should always renew; without a one-time option nobody can accidentally pay a single month and lapse.
  • No separate "Billing" question is needed on the form — the member picks the frequency right in the payment section, and it recurs automatically.
  • If the amount field the gateway adds has its own label, that label replaces "Membership Amount" — fine either way, as long as its help text points at the pricing table.
  • If option prices / auto-totaling is offered on choice fields, skip it — the price depends on household size, so the typed amount stays the source of truth.
The amount recurs: whatever number the member types is what the gateway bills every cycle, forever. That's why step 9 has the office verify each new membership's amount against the pricing table — a typo would otherwise repeat every month.

8

Test before announcing

  • Submit a real test: Single · Basic, amount $1, monthly recurrence, with "TEST" in Anything Else. Then in the gateway: confirm a recurring subscription was actually created (not a one-time charge), cancel it, and refund the $1.
  • Open the notification email: every field you filled is there, labels read cleanly, nothing landed under the wrong name.
  • Open the receipt: branded layout intact (if it arrives as raw code, the HTML got line-wrapped — re-paste it), subject line correct.
  • Submit once more as Family · Chai choosing annual billing in the payment section, with spouse and 2 children filled in — confirm every spouse and child row appears in the email and receipt, the singles-only test had none, and the gateway shows a yearly subscription. Cancel and refund it too.
  • Fill it once on a phone. Everything reachable, nothing clipped.
9

When a membership arrives

The office routine for each notification email:

  1. Check for missing family details first: a Couple signup without spouse rows, or a Family signup whose children rows don't match the number of children — reply and collect what's missing before anything else. Spouse and children information is mandatory when they exist.
  2. Verify the amount and frequency in the gateway — members type their own number, and whatever they typed now recurs automatically. Check the new subscription against the pricing table for their level and household (monthly amount on monthly billing, 12x on annual). If it's off, correct it in the gateway and let them know.
  3. Reply to welcome the member — the receipt tells them to expect a personal note from the office.
  4. Calendar the special days — birthdays, anniversary, children — onto the community calendar.
  5. Read Anything Else for yahrzeits, donor wall wording, or questions, and pass them to the Rabbi.
